1. Executive Summary

Pool Master Counter is a live pool/billiards scoreboard, stats, and tournament app that runs entirely in the browser — no server, no account, no build step — with a native iOS companion app that has already shipped a real, working monetization mechanism.

The product is not a prototype or a mockup: it is a complete, working application in active use today, covering live scoring for every common game type, single/double-elimination and round-robin tournaments, automatic Elo-style player ratings, ten accessible themes, and full localization into English, French, Spanish, and Cantonese. It has near-zero marginal cost to run — the web app is a static site with no backend or database.

Unlike most early-stage pitches, the monetization path is not hypothetical: a native Apple StoreKit 2 in-app purchase already unlocks a premium feature on iOS today, alongside a rewarded-ad alternative for players who'd rather not pay. This plan lays out the product, the market it serves, how it makes money, and what it needs to grow.

2. Problem & Opportunity

Casual and league pool is played every night in bars, clubs, pool halls, and home tables. Today, the people playing it are stuck between three bad options:

  • Paper and memory. No history, no stats, nothing to look back on, easy to lose track of who actually won.
  • Generic tally-counter apps. They count up and down but have no memory of who played, no ratings, and no tournament structure.
  • Heavyweight league-management platforms. Built for league administrators and organizations, not for a casual group starting a game — often requiring accounts, league membership, or per-venue subscription pricing (see Competitive Landscape).

Pool Master Counter sits in the gap: the zero-friction setup of a scoring app, with the real stats and tournament structure of a league platform — usable by two friends at a bar table exactly as well as by a weekly tournament night organizer.

3. Product Overview

The product ships today as a free, installable web app (PWA) and a native iOS app built on the same codebase via Capacitor. Core capabilities:

  • Setup Wizard — a five-step flow that gets any table playing in under a minute, no account required.
  • Live scoreboard — tap-to-score for 8-Ball, 9-Ball, Straight Pool, One Pocket, and more, with automatic win detection and milestone celebrations.
  • Tournaments — single elimination, double elimination, and round robin, with live brackets and standings.
  • Career stats & ratings — every player gets a win/loss history, head-to-head records, and an automatic Elo-style rating inspired by the scale FargoRate (the system behind USA Pool League) publishes.
  • Day Reports — one-tap shareable summaries of a night's play, ready to text, email, or copy.
  • Ten themes, four languages — dark, bright, and high-contrast palettes with WCAG-legible color computation; full parity across English, French, Spanish, and Cantonese.
  • Local-first data — everything lives in the browser's local storage on-device. No server, no account, nothing transmitted, with full JSON export/import for backup and device transfer.

4. Market

Pool and billiards are played casually and competitively worldwide — in bars and pubs with informal leagues, in dedicated pool halls and clubs, and on home tables among regular groups. The target users span a spectrum:

  • Casual players who just want an honest score kept without pen and paper.
  • Regular groups and home-table owners who play often enough to want stats and history.
  • Bar and pub leagues and weekly tournament nights that currently rely on informal tracking or a paid league platform.
  • Pool halls and clubs looking for a lightweight way to run a tournament night without adopting heavyweight venue-management software.

6. Business Model

The monetization approach is freemium, and — unlike most early-stage plans — it is already built and functioning on the native iOS app:

  • Free core product. The full scoring, stats, and tournament engine stays free on the web app, which is how the product has earned its users so far.
  • Native in-app purchase. A one-time Pro unlock, implemented as a real Apple StoreKit 2 purchase, removes the paywall on premium features on the native iOS app — starting with the Colorful Report export.
  • Rewarded-ad alternative. Players who'd rather not pay can unlock the same premium feature for a single session by watching a short rewarded ad — a familiar, low-friction mobile monetization pattern wired into the same gate.
  • Expandable gate. The unlock check wraps any feature in one line of code. Deciding what stays free versus Pro is a product decision, not an engineering one — new premium features can ship behind the same infrastructure with no rework.
  • Near-zero cost to serve. The web app is a static site with no backend, database, or per-user infrastructure cost — gross margin on the free tier is effectively 100%, so growth in the free tier doesn't add to cost, and every converted user drops straight to the bottom line.

7. Go-to-Market Strategy

The zero-friction, no-account design of the free web app is itself a distribution advantage: anyone can start using it from a link with no signup, which lowers the barrier for word-of-mouth spread within a bar, league, or friend group compared to products that require an account or league membership up front.

  1. Community-first distribution. Pool is a social game played in identifiable venues and communities (bars, pool halls, leagues) — direct outreach to league organizers and venues, plus organic sharing of Day Reports (which double as a lightweight invitation to try the app), are the primary near-term channels.
  2. App Store presence. The native iOS app extends reach to users who prefer an installed app and enables the StoreKit monetization path; an Android build via the same Capacitor codebase is a natural extension (see Roadmap).
  3. Localization as a wedge. Full parity across English, French, Spanish, and Cantonese already positions the product for non-English-first pool communities without additional engineering lift.

8. Technology & IP

  • Dependency-free web app. Plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript, no framework, no build step — the entire app is a static site, making it cheap to host, easy to audit, and resilient to framework churn.
  • Cross-platform from one codebase. The native iOS app is built via Capacitor, wrapping the same web codebase with native plugins (StoreKit purchases) added where the platform requires it.
  • Custom rating engine. The Elo-style player rating is a from-scratch implementation matched to FargoRate's published odds and scale — not a licensed or reverse-engineered dependency.
  • Automated test coverage. A Selenium/pytest browser suite covers the web app end-to-end; a parallel XCUITest suite covers the native iOS app, including the StoreKit purchase flow.

13. Risks & Mitigations

  • Platform dependency risk. The native monetization path depends on Apple's App Store policies and fee structure. Mitigation: the free web app is fully independent of any app store and continues to function regardless of iOS platform changes.
  • Conversion risk. Freemium pool apps are unproven at scale for this specific model. Mitigation: the rewarded-ad path gives a second, lower-friction monetization route alongside the direct purchase, and the paywall gate is cheap to re-target at different features.
  • Competitive risk. Named competitors (Section 5) have existing users and, in some cases, official league relationships (e.g. APA). Mitigation: Pool Master Counter's zero-account, zero-cost entry point targets the large casual segment those products don't serve well.
  • Distribution risk. As a bootstrapped, community-distributed product, growth may be slower than a paid-acquisition strategy. Mitigation: near-zero hosting cost means the product can sustain a long organic growth runway without burning capital on infrastructure.
